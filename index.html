Design Decision
Oil Change Exclusion — What It Is and What It Misses
A deliberate marketing choice to surface pure non-oil-change demand — with a documented tradeoff

Before any classification occurs, the query identifies and removes entire transactions that contain an oil change line item. The exclusion is at the transaction level — if any line item on a visit matches an oil change pattern, the entire transaction is dropped, including any other services on that same visit.

ITEM_TYPE codes excluded:

OIL OL1 OL6 OF SYNTHETIC OIL FILTERS ENGINE OIL FS04 ENGINE OIL FS01 REC

ITEM_DESCR text patterns excluded:

LIKE %OIL CHANGE% LIKE %OIL & FILTER% LIKE %OIL AND FILTER% LIKE %OIL/FILTER%
Marketing decision note — known gap: This exclusion was intentional to produce a pure non-oil service demand view. However, quick-lube oil change visits frequently include upsold services — air filters, wiper blades, battery tests, fluid top-offs — that are bundled within the same transaction. Under this methodology, those services are not counted. Estimated gap: approximately 34,000–52,000 additional classified service observations may exist within the excluded oil-change transactions (applying a 40–60% bundled upsell rate to the 86,504 excluded transactions). This represents a potential undercounting of 26–39% relative to the current 132,021 baseline. Categories most likely understated: Air Filters (engine + cabin), Wiper Blades, Battery (testing/service), and Fluids & Cooling top-offs. A future analysis option is to run classification across all transactions and compare resulting store-level service mixes to quantify the actual gap.
Section 4 — Service Hierarchy
Classification Architecture
CASE/WHEN on UPPER(ITEM_DESCR) — first matching rule wins — 15 L1 categories, 55 L2 subcategories

Classification is implemented entirely within the Snowflake SQL query as a multi-step CASE/WHEN block operating on the ITEM_DESCR field of TRANSACTIONS_DETAILS — a free-text POS field populated by store technicians with a mix of SKU codes, product names, and written descriptions. There is no controlled vocabulary. Two parallel passes run on the same UPPER(ITEM_DESCR) input: one for L1_CATEGORY and a separate, more granular pass for L2_SUBCATEGORY. More specific patterns always appear before broader ones.

Section 4.3 — Subcategory Detail
L2 Subcategory Logic
Key examples of specificity ordering within L1 categories
Battery — 4-tier resolution
  1. Battery Replacement — DieHard SKU codes (DHGOL, DHSVR, AGM PLAT) and T-number codes match first
  2. Battery Fees — STATE BATTERY, TEXAS BATTERY match second
  3. Starting & Charging — ALTERNATOR, STARTER match third
  4. Battery Service — broad %BATTERY% catch-all matches last
Brakes — directional before general
  1. Brake Pads — Front — FRONT BRAKE PAD, FRONT PADS, FRONT BRAKES
  2. Brake Pads — Rear — REAR BRAKE PAD, REAR PADS, REAR BRAKES
  3. Rotors — Front/Rear — directional ROTOR patterns
  4. Brake Fluid — DOT 3, DOT 4, BRAKE FLUID
  5. Brake Pads (General) — broad BRAKE PAD catch-all last
Differentials — 3 subcategories
  1. Front Differential — explicit FRONT DIFFERENTIAL text
  2. Rear Differential — explicit REAR DIFFERENTIAL text
  3. Gear Oil — catch-all: GL-5, GEAR OIL, GEAR LUBE, AXLE OIL, viscosity grades (75W, 80W), GEARBOX, LIMITED SLIP, DIFF FLUID
Fuel System — 2 distinct services
  1. Fuel System Cleaning — INJECTOR CLEAN, THROTTLE BODY CLEAN, BG 44K, BG44K, FUEL INDUCTION, CARBON CLEAN, GDI FUEL
  2. Fuel Filter — FUEL FILTER plus F-number SKU pattern
Section 5 — Ops Cross-Validation
Cross-Validation Against Operations Data
Our transaction model vs. Brian Brooks' annual store capability matrix — 285 stores compared, four gaps closed

In March 2026 the service hierarchy was cross-validated against the operations capability matrix maintained by Brian Brooks (Director, Store Operations). 285 of 286 stores appeared in both datasets. The comparison identified systematic SQL pattern gaps and produced four material improvements to the classification logic (v2).

What each dataset measures:

Reconciliation process for each service category:

Each service was mapped to its closest ops equivalent. Stores were compared on a YES/NO basis. Where "They YES / We NO" exceeded 10 stores: (1) pull raw ITEM_DESCR values for the gap stores from Snowflake; (2) identify missing patterns; (3) add RLIKE or LIKE clauses to both L1 and L2 CASE/WHEN blocks; (4) re-run query and recount.
